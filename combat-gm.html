Combat

Raids · Encounters · Weapons · Fortifications · Casualties · Faction Intelligence

// The Attack Roll
Roll D20 + Attack Value + Weapon Bonus + Modifiers. Subtract the defender's Defence Value. The result is the margin — which band it falls in determines the outcome for that round.
// Combat Scale
Combat resolves at group level. Each side rolls once per group per round. Individual weapon stats are averaged across the group. A group can be any size from 2 to the full force.
// When Combat Triggers
Raid: A faction attacks a settlement.
Expedition Encounter: Scouts meet hostiles in the field.
Assault: A faction attacks another faction's location.
Skirmish: Fewer than 6 fighters per side.
// Time Cost
Raid defence: 0 seasons. Assault on a defended position: 0.5 seasons. Siege: 1+ seasons. Fighters in combat roles cannot produce resources that season.

Round Sequence
1
Declare
Both sides secretly choose Attack, Hold, or Withdraw. Reveal simultaneously.
2
Initiative
Both roll D20. Higher acts first. Tie: defender first. Scouts +3 in field encounters. Ambush: attacker goes first automatically.
3
Ranged
LR bolt-action first, then rifles and SMGs, then pistols and bows. Apply casualties before melee resolves.
4
Melee
Both sides roll simultaneously. Hold groups add +2 DEF to all allies at same position and absorb the first allied casualty each round.
5
Morale
Any side taking 30%+ casualties this round rolls Morale Check (D20 + modifier). Failure: group Withdraws next round.
6
Repeat
Max 6 rounds before mutual exhaustion — treat as a draw. Both sides retreat.
Morale
// Morale Check Triggers
30%+ casualties in one round · Leader killed · Settlement breached · 50%+ total casualties in the engagement.
// Morale Roll
D20 + modifier. 10+: hold. 7–9: fall back, lose Hold bonus. 1–6: full Withdraw.

Modifiers: Militia +3 · Scouts +2 · Conscripts −2 · Palisade Wall +2 · Leader alive +2 · Outnumbered 2:1+ −3
// Raider Morale (GM)
If raiders take 40%+ total casualties, GM rolls D20. On 1–8 they break and flee. Pursuit option: send Scouts, roll D20 — Hit or better recovers D6×5 resources from their abandoned supplies.
// Surrender
Either side may surrender at round start. No further casualties. Prisoners cost 1 food + 1 water per season. Options: ransom to their faction, recruit, or release.
Unit Types
⚔ Militia
ATK +3 DEF +4 Morale +3
Trained defenders. Full combat effectiveness in all roles. Starting allocation: 12. Expand to 18 for high-raid environments.
🗺 Scouts
ATK +2 DEF +2 Initiative +3
Mobile and fast. +3 initiative in field encounters. Best for ambush, flanking, pursuit. Cannot hold fortified positions without −2 DEF penalty.
⚙ Conscripted Workers
ATK +0 DEF +1 Morale −2
Untrained. Only useful behind a fortification bonus. If over 50% of force are conscripts, all morale rolls −3. Never use in field combat roles.
⚠ Scholar Safety: If settlement is breached, roll D6 per Scholar: on a 1 they are caught in the fighting and roll on the Injury track. A Masterwork LR bolt-action can target a named Scholar before combat — Nat 20 required.
// Weapon Assignment
Weapons assigned to groups, not individuals. A group's stat is the most common weapon type. Mixed groups use the lower bonus.
// Range Bands
Close: pistols, melee, SMGs, shortbow · Medium: bolt-actions, shortbow, longbow · Full: semi-auto rifles, longbow · Extreme: LR bolt-action only. Outside range: −4 to roll.
// Jam Rules
Scavenged ammo: D10 per group per round. 1 = jam (fighter can't attack). Crude: 1–2. Clearing costs next action. Bullet Press ammo: no jams.
// Ammo Per Round
Pistol: 2 small · SMG: 6 small · Bolt: 1 large · Semi-auto: 3 large · LR bolt: 1 large · Bow: 3 arrows. Group that runs out drops to melee.

// Defence Value
Every defending group has base DEF from unit type plus fortification bonuses.

Militia: DEF +4 · Scouts: DEF +2 · Conscripts: DEF +1
// Perimeter Layers
Settlements have an Outer and Inner perimeter. Raiders must breach the outer before engaging defenders directly. A Critical on a perimeter position breaches it — cannot be repaired mid-combat.
// Hold Formation
Hold groups do not attack but add +2 DEF to all allies at their position and absorb the first allied casualty each round.
// Night Raids
Night raids: all attack rolls −2 for both sides. Watchtower Network negates this for defenders completely.
Fortification Buildings
Palisade Wall (Tier I)
+4 DEF outer perimeter defenders. −20% raid damage if perimeter holds.
Watchtower Network (Tier II)
+2 warning seasons. Negates night penalty. +2 initiative all raids.
Scout Training Grounds (Tier III)
Scouts +1 ATK, +1 DEF. Flanking action removes defender's fortification bonus for 1 round.
Reinforced Bunker Extension (Tier IV)
+6 DEF inner perimeter. Requires 2 Crits to breach. Defenders: +2 morale.
Prion Barrier / Burn Wall (Tier III)
Attackers crossing roll D6: 1–2 = 1 automatic Injury. +2 DEF outer perimeter.
Reinforced Vault Storage (Tier IV)
Stored resources immune to raider looting even if settlement is breached.

// Casualties Are Permanent
Every fighter lost is a worker, farmer, or defender gone permanently. Combat is meant to feel costly — a raid won with 8 dead is a crisis that will take seasons to recover from.
// Casualty Tracks
After combat, all casualties sort into: Injured (recover), Severely Injured (may die without treatment), and Killed (permanent). Deduct Killed from population immediately.
// Medical Buildings
None: Severely Injured — 50% death chance/season.
Field Surgery Station (T1): −25% injury-to-death. Treats 5/season.
Advanced Surgery Theatre (T3): −50%. Treats 15/season. Severe: 0.5 seasons recovery.
// Settlement Morale
After any raid or assault: −2 morale for 2 seasons. Decisively repelled with under 10% casualties: +2 morale instead.

☠ GM RESTRICTED — Do not share with players. Reveals faction combat stats, weapon inventories, aggression ratings, and strategic weaknesses. Disclose only through successful scouting, NPC contact, or traded intelligence.
// How to Use Faction Stats
Strength = total combat power rating (1–10). Aggression = likelihood of acting each season (roll D6 at season end — if result ≤ Aggression, faction acts). Full stats only known through successful scouting.
// Faction Actions
When a faction acts (Aggression roll succeeds): D6 — 1–2: raid nearest settlement · 3–4: expand territory or claim a resource site · 5: trade demand or tribute request · 6: diplomatic contact.
// Respawn & Recovery
Faction losing 60%+ fighters enters Fractured state — no actions for 2 seasons, then rebuilds at 40% strength per season. Exception: warlord factions (Reapers) — kill the warlord and the faction dissolves permanently.
// Intel Decay
After 3 seasons without scouting a faction, their fighter count and weapon tier revert to Unknown — they may have grown or rearmed. Broadcast Tower partially tracks radio-using factions.
The Reapers
Overview Warlord Faction
Pure raider gang. No interest in building — entirely parasitic. Base at the outer perimeter of Ground Zero — the city ruins at the map centre. They do not go deep into the city (prion risk), but they control access to it and extract resources from the outer industrial ring. Leader: "Crowbar" — kills deserters personally. If Crowbar is killed (named casualty from a Nat 20), the faction dissolves permanently within 2 seasons.
Strength: 7 · Aggression: 5 · Fighters: ~35 · ATK: +3 · DEF: +2 · Morale: +1 (collapses if Crowbar falls)
Weapons: Mostly Standard. 3–4 Reliable SMGs held by inner circle only. Scavenged ammo — jam rules apply in any fight over 3 rounds.
Weakness: No Bullet Press. Ammo runs out in prolonged fights. Draw it past 4 rounds and their attack rolls start dropping by −1 per round as ammo degrades.
Overview Organised Commune
The Covenant. Agricultural base with a defensive posture. Rural farmland cluster with partial palisade walls and an Elder Council of three — cannot be decapitated with a single kill. Will negotiate if approached peacefully and given time.
Strength: 5 · Aggression: 2 · Fighters: ~22 · ATK: +2 · DEF: +5 (fortified farmstead) · Morale: +4
Weapons: Standard bolt-actions (hunting rifles). A few Reliable bolt-actions from pre-collapse stock. Moderate large bullet reserves — will become ammo-critical in a sustained fight beyond 4 rounds.
Opportunity: Food surplus and live animals. They want medicine and Salvaged Books. Trade routes with them are highly valuable.
Overview Maritime Faction
The Drowned. Controls coastal access and submerged city dive rights. Only operational boats on the map. Fortified harbour district. Leader: "The Captain" — charismatic and calculating. Prefers leverage over direct conflict. Charges tolls for sea access. Will not fight wars they can't win.
Strength: 6 · Aggression: 2 · Fighters: ~28 · ATK: +3 · DEF: +4 (harbour fortifications) · Morale: +3
Weapons: Reliable across the board. 2 Masterwork semi-autos. Working Bullet Press (small bullets). Large ammo is their weak point — target that in any prolonged engagement.
Opportunity: The only reliable large-scale source of copper wire. They know this and price it accordingly. They want Detection Lab access, medicine, and large bullets.
Overview Wasteland Nomads
The Silent. Prion-exposed survivors. Nomadic cells of 4–8. Operate in zones no other faction will enter. Not organised — each cell is autonomous. Do not respond to diplomatic contact through normal channels.

Prion Wound Rule: Any fighter wounded by The Silent rolls D6: on a 1 they receive a prion exposure. Detection Lab check required on return.
Strength: 3 · Aggression: 3 · Fighters: ~15 total (cells of 4–8) · ATK: +1 · DEF: +1 · Morale: +2 (prion exposure made them fearless)
Weapons: Crude and Standard melee/pistols. One cell has a Standard bolt-action.
Opportunity: They carry prion samples passively. With careful contact, trade 1–3 samples per season. Worth far more than fighting them.
Overview Government Remnant
The Remnant Authority. Sealed hardened bunker complex. Bureaucratic and disciplined. Will attempt to absorb weaker settlements diplomatically first — military action is their last resort, but when they commit, they commit fully. Leader: "The Director" — former civil servant with access to pre-collapse blueprint records. Demands 10% tribute per season from settlements they consider within their jurisdiction.
Strength: 9 · Aggression: 2 · Fighters: ~55 · ATK: +4 · DEF: +7 (hardened bunker) · Morale: +5
Weapons: Reliable standard-issue, 8–10 Masterwork rifles. Working Bullet Press (both bullet types). Active Gun Bench — ongoing weapon production each season.
Special: Cannot be destroyed by conventional raids. Always signals 2 seasons in advance before any military action. Offers blueprint copies (T4–T5) in exchange for loyalty pledges. Treat as the endgame pressure — not another raider. The Director actively seeks the government archive at Ground Zero. Each season at Aggression roll success they send a scouting party toward the city. If they reach the archive before the players, they acquire its contents and their Strength increases to 11.

🤝 GM CONTEXT — Trade & Relationship System. These rules govern all inter-faction dealings including player factions. The relationship scale, negotiation rolls, and deal types apply equally to player settlements. The faction-specific wants and offers here supplement the combat intelligence in the Factions tab.
// What Can Be Traded
Any tangible resource, building output, or service. Resources have base Trade Values (TV). Blueprints, Scholars, and unique items have negotiated values.
Cannot trade: Population (triggers permanent −15 relationship with all who learn of it) · prion-contaminated goods without disclosure · promises that cannot be mechanically kept.
// Trade Goods Buildings
Some buildings produce Trade Goods — processed outputs valued above raw equivalents.
Fermentation & Brewing: +12 goods/season
Map Printing Press: +8 goods/season
Coastal Trading Post: +20 goods/season
Document Printing Press: +15 goods/season
// The Negotiation Roll
Proposing a trade or agreement: roll D20 + Relationship Modifier.
Bonuses: Scholar on diplomatic team: +2. Coastal Trading Post with maritime factions: +3. The result determines whether they accept, counter-offer, or refuse.
// How Relationships Change
Moves slowly through consistent actions. Two completed fair trades = +1 tier. Two consecutive missed deliveries = −2 tiers.
Killing a faction leader or breaking a sealed deal = immediate −3 tiers. Significant betrayals reach other factions within 2 seasons. Protecting a faction in combat can jump +1 tier immediately.

What Each Faction Wants and Offers
The Reapers Default: Hostile
They want: Food (any) · Scrap Metal · Salvaged Medicine · Small Bullets · Other factions' defence intel
They offer: Raided settlement goods · Large bullets looted · Crude firearms · Wasteland location intel

GM note: The Reapers rarely trade in good faith. Any negotiation is likely a delay tactic before raiding. A Cautious or above relationship requires Crowbar's personal approval — roleplay as grudging and transactional.
The Covenant Default: Wary
They want: Salvaged Medicine · Salvaged Books · Medicinal Seeds · Chemical Reagents · Prion-safe water intelligence
They offer: Food surplus · Live animals (tested) · Hides · Seeds · Large bullets from hunting stock · Safe rural passage

GM note: The Covenant's Elder Council votes on all deals — takes one additional season to confirm major agreements. They are the most reliable long-term partners if trust is established. They will share food in a genuine crisis even at Wary.
The Drowned Default: Neutral
They want: Detection Lab access (rented) · Salvaged Medicine · Chemical Reagents · Stone and Timber · Large bullets
They offer: Copper Wire · Copper Pipe · Maritime Blueprints T3–T4 · Salt · Fish · Sea passage rights · Star Chart copies

GM note: The Captain knows they hold a monopoly on copper wire and prices it accordingly. They will not sell star charts until at least Cautious. At Friendly, The Captain may offer to escort player vessels through contested water — a significant military asset.
The Silent Default: Neutral
They want: Food (desperately) · Clean Water · Salvaged Medicine · Shelter in winter · Amnesty from prion protocols
They offer: Prion Samples (reliable supply) · Wasteland salvage · Hides and Pelts · Faction movement intel · Crude firearms

GM note: Contact must be initiated carefully — approaching a Silent cell in wasteland without prior agreement triggers a combat check. The Silent live inside the city ruins — the only faction that operates freely in Ground Zero. They know the interior layout that no other faction does, including the location of the government archive. This knowledge is tradeable. Once contact is established they are loyal suppliers of prion samples — city strain samples count as 2 Research Points each. They respond well to being treated as people rather than a biohazard.
Remnant Authority Default: Neutral, tribute expected
They want: 10% surplus tribute per season · Loyalty declarations · Prion Samples · Skilled workers or Scholars
They offer: Blueprint copies T2–T4 · Large bullet supply · Protection guarantees · Pre-collapse records · Formal territory rights

GM note: Tribute can be paid in low-value goods (stone, timber, sand) to satisfy the requirement mechanically while protecting high-value stockpiles. Friendly reduces tribute to 5%. Allied eliminates it in exchange for other loyalty commitments. The Director responds very positively to offers of Scholars for apprenticeship — this is their primary interest beyond tribute.
