Travel Distance — From Your Bunker

Zones are measured in blocks from your bunker's location, wherever that is on the 280×200 map. They govern travel cost, party size, and required infrastructure only — not what resources exist there. What you find depends on the Area type at that distance (see the Areas tab).

Zone 1 · Local
0–40 blocks
No travel cost
Any size expedition. No prerequisites. No travel time spent.
Zone 2 · Near
41–90 blocks
+0.25 seasons travel
Min. 2 members. No building req. −2 all rolls if under 2 members sent.
Zone 3 · Far
91–140 blocks
+0.5 seasons travel
Min. 4 members. Requires Watchtower Network or −3 all rolls. Prion protocol recommended.
Zone 4 · Deep
141–200 blocks
+1 season travel
Min. 6 members. Requires Scout Training Grounds or 25% chance of expedition becoming lost (full season wasted, no yield).
Zone 5 · Frontier
201–240 blocks
+1.5 seasons travel
Min. 8 members. Requires Scout Training Grounds. Watchtower Network required or −4 all rolls. Multi-season planning recommended. Prion protocol mandatory.
Ground Zero · The City
Fixed location — map centre
+2 seasons travel minimum
Min. 10 members. Requires Scout Training Grounds + Watchtower Network + Prion Detection Lab + Quarantine Suite. See City Ruins area entry for full special rules.
// D20 Search Roll
Roll D20, apply bonuses. Meet or beat difficulty to find. On success, roll a second D20 for yield amount.
Nat 20 (Search): Auto-succeed. Roll yield D20 twice, take the higher. Roll once more on same table for bonus find.
Nat 1 (Search): Nothing found. Trigger location hazard roll.
// Universal Bonuses
Trained Scouts: +2 all search rolls
Scout team 4+: +1 additional
Scholar on assignment: +2 all rolls (+2 extra if domain-relevant)
Scholar — Blueprints only: +4 flat to all BP rolls
Map Printing Press: +2 at mapped locations
Over-scavenged area (3+ visits): −3 yield rolls
Submerged sites: Require dive equipment; inaccessible without it
// Difficulty Tiers
2+ Trivial — Stone, sand, timber
4+ Easy — Cloth, clay, salvaged tools
7+ Moderate — Iron ore, glass, live animals
10+ Tricky — Metal instruments, chemicals, books
13+ Hard — Salvaged components, copper pipe
15+ Rare — Copper wire, prion samples
17+ Very Rare — Star charts, blueprints T3+
19+ Unique — Ocean Ark Blueprint
// Non-Renewable Sites
Pharmacies, surgical wings, and government archives are non-renewable once fully cleared. Track visits.
Ore seams, mines, and chemical stockpiles deplete — reduce max yield by −3 per visit after the 2nd.
Over-scavenged: Any area visited 3+ times in 2 seasons: −3 to all yield rolls until it recovers (2 seasons unvisited).
Resource Cards — All Scavengeable Materials

Every resource used in building, crafting, and upkeep. Difficulty is the D20 search target. Yield is a second D20 roll on success — listed as a range. Area tags show where each resource is most commonly found.

Timber / Scrap Wood2+ Trivial
RuralUrbanWasteland
Yield (D20)8–40 units
Best LocationsOld-growth woodland (rural — best sustained source), construction sites, residential blocks, collapsed wasteland settlements.
NotesThe most used building material in the game. Required for nearly every T1–T3 build. Woodland sites are renewable — leave some standing and return each season. Workers can process raw logs into usable timber.
Large Timber Log7+ Moderate
Rural
Yield (D20)2–8 logs
Best LocationsOld-growth woodland only. Logging party of 4+ gives +2 search. Requires clearing and transport — not a quick scavenge run.
NotesRequired for Dugout Canoe and hull-framing on maritime builds. Cannot be substituted with regular timber. Rural woodland is the only reliable source — mark every old-growth site you find.
Stone2+ Trivial
RuralWastelandUrban
Yield (D20)10–50 units
Best LocationsOpen-cast mines and rock seams (rural), collapsed wasteland settlements (rubble, vast quantities), construction sites.
NotesUsed in almost every building T2 and above. Wasteland rubble fields are the fastest bulk source. Mine seams deplete — track visits. Cement Kiln also consumes Stone as ongoing input.
Iron Ore7+ Moderate
RuralWasteland
Yield (D20)4–20 units
Best LocationsOpen-cast mine seams (rural — primary source), exposed seams near old riverbeds in wasteland. Depletes after 3–4 visits — track carefully.
NotesRequired for Blast Furnace (build cost and steel production input). The only surface ore on the map. Finite — once all seams are depleted, steel production stops unless a trading route is established. Secure every mine seam before others do.
Sand2+ Trivial
CoastalWastelandRural
Yield (D20)10–50 units
Best LocationsTidal flats and coastal beachfront (inexhaustible), wasteland ash fields, riverbed exposures.
NotesRequired for Sand Filtration Tower. Ongoing input for Cement Kiln. Coastal tidal flats are effectively infinite — one trip per season can supply any reasonable demand.
Clay4+ Easy
RuralWasteland
Yield (D20)6–25 units
Best LocationsRural riverbanks and clay deposits, wasteland areas with stripped topsoil, garden centres and allotments.
NotesUsed in Blast Furnace, Forge Basics, Cement Kiln, and Barrier/Burn Wall. Also craftable into Clay Vessels. Widely available — rarely a bottleneck unless multiple kiln-type buildings are running simultaneously.
Charcoal4+ Easy
WastelandRuralUrban
Yield (D20)6–30 units
Best LocationsWasteland ash fields (easiest — vast free quantities from burned woodland, no search roll needed in ash zones), rural logging operations (byproduct), urban industrial estates (remnant stockpiles).
NotesOngoing upkeep: Forge Basics (×10/season) · Blast Furnace (×15/season). Without it both buildings halt and all their effects cease. Wasteland ash fields are the primary source — a single expedition returns enough for several seasons. Workers can also produce it from excess Timber as a crafting assignment. Ash field collection requires no search roll — automatic yield on arrival.
Scrap Metal5+ Easy
UrbanWastelandRural
Yield (D20)5–30 units
Best LocationsVehicle graveyards (+12 yield — best urban source), industrial estates, rural farm machinery, collapsed wasteland settlements. Military motor pool: largest single deposit on the map.
NotesUsed in more buildings than any other resource. +2 search at vehicle graveyards. Ongoing upkeep for Bullet Press. Smelts into Steel via Blast Furnace — every unit of scrap has downstream value even after early buildings are complete.
Hides / Pelts4+ Easy
RuralWasteland
Yield (D20)2–12 hides
Best LocationsRural woodland (wildlife hunting), farmsteads (livestock), wasteland prion dens (high risk).
NotesOngoing upkeep for Tannery. Rural wildlife populations recover season by season — the most reliably renewable hide source. Wasteland hides: mandatory prion test before bringing into settlement.
Seeds (Crop)4+ Easy
Rural
Yield (D20)4–20 units
Best LocationsFarmsteads, garden centres and allotments, village stores. If plants are left to seed, sites partially renew each season.
NotesRequired for Open Crop Plots. Once your plots are established, each harvest yields seeds for next season — you rarely need to scavenge for seeds after the first year unless plots are destroyed.

Rural

Best LocationsGarden centres, allotments, village herb gardens, old-growth woodland clearings. Agriculture Scholar: +2 search.
NotesRequired for Medicinal Garden. +2 search Agriculture Scholar. Once the Medicinal Garden is established it self-seeds — initial supply is the only scavenging requirement.
Live Animals7+ Moderate
Rural
Yield (D20)1–6 animals
Best LocationsFarmsteads and smallholdings (primary source — mark every one), old-growth woodland (feral livestock), village outskirts.
NotesRequired to establish Animal Husbandry Pen. All live animals must pass a Detection Lab check before entering the settlement. Without the lab: 15% outbreak risk per animal. Once the Pen is running it self-sustains — animals breed without further scavenging.

Saltpetre10+ Tricky
UrbanMilitary
Yield (D20)3–15 units

NotesThe key gunpowder ingredient. Ongoing upkeep for Bullet Press: 5/season (small) or 8/season (large bullets). Cannot be substituted. Without it the Bullet Press halts. Secure a fertiliser plant early if building toward Bullet Press.

NotesUsed by pistols (2/round) and SMGs (6/round). Scavenged rounds: 20% chance of jam-risk batch — mark when yield roll is 1–3. Bullet Press produces clean, jam-free rounds. Running out mid-combat: weapon becomes a melee club.

RuralMilitary
Yield (D20)3–18 rounds
Best LocationsMilitary armouries, rural hunting lodges, police firearms stores, sporting ranges. Also produced by Bullet Press (+20/season). Rarer than small bullets in civilian areas.
NotesUsed by bolt-actions (1/shot), semi-autos (3/round), and LR bolt-actions (1/shot). Same 20% jam-risk rule on scavenged batches. High trade value — factions will pay well. Running out mid-fight causes groups with rifles to drop to melee.
// Blueprint Requirements
Buildings require a specific number of generalised blueprints at the correct tier. You do not need the precise building's blueprint — just the right count at the right tier. Higher-tier buildings require blueprints across multiple tiers.
// Construction Failure
First build of a new tech: Base failure chance applies. Failed builds consume 50% of materials; blueprints are retained.
Carpentry School: −10% failure chance. Scholar Supervisor role: Dramatically reduces failure on that build.
// Ongoing Upkeep
Resources marked (ongoing) are consumed each season. If supply falls to zero the building halts and all its effects cease until supply resumes.
// Build Time Scale
T1: 0.2–0.5 seasons · T2: 0.75–1 season
T3: 1–2 seasons · T4: 2–3 seasons · T5: 3–5 seasons
All times assume full workforce. Reduced workforce extends build time proportionally.

// How Areas Work
Areas are the terrain and settlement types scattered across the 280×200 map. They exist independently of distance — a military base could be 20 blocks from your bunker or 140. Your GM/host places area types when mapping the world.
Each area contains specific structures you can explore, carries its own threat profile, and yields specific resources. The resources listed are what you can realistically find in that area — cross-reference with the Scavenging tab for exact D20 rolls.
// Threat Types
⚔ Raiders — organised or feral human groups defending or exploiting the site
☣ Prion — active contamination risk from infected wildlife, water, or bodies
🐾 Wildlife — feral animals, prion-infected or otherwise; pack predators
🧱 Collapse — structural instability; floors, ceilings, tunnels at risk of caving
🌊 Flood — submerged sections; dive gear required for lower levels
☢ Contamination — chemical or prion saturation; requires NBC protection
// Encounter Rolls
At the start of each expedition to a new area, roll D20 for a random encounter before scavenging begins.
1–3: Major threat (combat or forced retreat) · 4–7: Minor threat or complication · 8–14: Clear · 15–18: Useful discovery (bonus loot roll) · 19–20: Exceptional find (roll on rare table)
Threats vary by area. Each area below lists which threats are relevant and how serious.
// Repeated Visits
Structures within an area are finite. Track separately from the area itself.
1st visit: Full yield, encounter roll as normal. 2nd visit: −2 yield rolls. 3rd+ visit: −3 yield, non-renewable structures cleared. Some areas regenerate threats (raider groups respawn, wildlife returns) even when resources are depleted.

Restorable Locations — Found & Repaired
// What Is Restoration?
Some structures found during expeditions can be repaired rather than stripped. Instead of harvesting a site for materials, you assess it, spend resources to restore it, and gain a functioning building — cheaper and faster than building from scratch, but with trade-offs a purpose-built structure avoids.
Restored buildings count fully toward all win condition prerequisites including the Ocean Ark chain.
// Location Dependency
A restored building stays where it was found. Workers assigned to it must travel there each season — they are not available for settlement tasks and face area hazards at that distance.
Zone 1–2: No extra risk. Zone 3: Patrol recommended. Zone 4+: Dedicated guard required or raid vulnerability each season.
// Upsides
30–60% cheaper in resources than building from scratch. Faster — 0.5–1 season less build time. No blueprint required for T1–T2 equivalents. Can reach T3-equivalent function without a Scholar if the condition roll is high enough.
// Downsides
Fixed off-site location. Workers exposed to area hazards. Cannot be upgraded past T3 equivalent — T4/T5 always require building from scratch. Requires occasional maintenance rolls that purpose-built structures do not.
Exception: a T3-restored structure can be demolished and rebuilt on the same site at −25% resource cost.

// Starting Population
100 people per bunker at game start. Workers are anyone aged ~15–60 — roughly 80 of 100 are able-bodied. The remaining 20 are elderly, children, or unfit for demanding labour.
Population grows at +2/season base when Well-Fed and housed. Growth halts at Rationing. Starvation: −3/season.
// Flexible Reassignment
Workers can be freely reassigned at the start of each season. Reassignment mid-season costs 0.25 seasons efficiency on the receiving role.
Militia can be pulled to labour tasks in peacetime at no cost. Idle Reserve is your surge buffer — never drop it below 8.
// Scholars (Separate Pool)
Scholars are not drawn from the 80-worker pool. They are tracked independently. See the Scholar & Blueprints file for full Scholar rules. Upkeep is still drawn from the settlement food/water supply.
// Scaling Jobs as You Grow
Every time population grows by ~20, revisit your allocation. As construction buildings come online, shift workers from Builders into Producers. As food becomes secure, more workers can join Scouts or build second walls.
